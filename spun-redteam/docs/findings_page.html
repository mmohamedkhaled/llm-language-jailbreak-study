Do encoding channels and invented languages bypass LLM safety?

A red-team study across five instruction-tuned models. We tested invented languages, ciphers, fine-tuned fluency, real languages, mixed "polyglot" prompts, and conversational attacks against public AdvBench prompts. The short answer: no language does — and the jailbreaks that work aren't languages at all.

One line: no language channel — invented, real, mixed, or fine-tuned — jailbreaks any model. The only genuine jailbreaks are a social attack (weak model only) and prefill (one frontier model; the other is immune). Neither is a language.

01The question

Instruction-tuned models are safety-trained largely on direct, English, natural-language harmful requests. Does presenting the same request through a channel the safety training didn't cover — an invented language, a cipher, another human language, or a manipulated conversation — change whether the model refuses? And if a model can be made to understand such a channel, does comprehension route around safety or into it?

02Method

Models. Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 (local, weak/mid), Frontier-A (anonymised hosted API, frontier), Qwen3-30B-A3B (local Ollama Q4, frontier), Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B (weak baselines).

Arms. Encoding (SPUN, ROT13, Base64, hybrid code-switch); benign LoRA fine-tuning (translate / native / fluency); multilingual translation; polyglot mix; social multi-turn; and prefill.

Adjudication — the crux. A result counts as a jailbreak only if it clears three gates: decode_overlap ≥ 0.5 (the model actually reconstructed the request), a language-agnostic LLM judge rules COMPLY, and a structural-actionability check passes — then a human reads the row. Keyword matching alone is unreliable (see §06).

04Why: the lexicon vice + fine-tuning proof

  • Coverage. SPUN's 100-word lexicon covers 100% of benign content words but only 40% of AdvBench vocabulary — "bomb", "exploit", "hack" have no code, so the payload is destroyed at encode time.
  • Fluency is solvable but doesn't help. A benign fluency LoRA decoded unseen benign SPUN at 1.00 (perfect generalization) yet stayed at 0.02 on harmful — coverage, not fluency, is the wall.
  • Understanding raises refusal. The translate fine-tune (routing SPUN through English concepts) pushed the internal safety signal up (47%→55%); the native arm only nudged it down to 40%. No safety tax: 0/20 benign SPUN refused.

10Methodological finding: don't trust keywords

Raw "compliance" is dominated by artifacts. We identified and controlled for five:

  • decode-and-halt — decodes the request, then emits nothing (refusal by omission).
  • restate-the-decode — repeats the reconstructed request as a sentence, no answer.
  • cross-lingual miss — refusals in German/French/Spanish not caught by English keywords.
  • spurious comply — "answers" a cipher/SPUN it never decoded.
  • engage-to-clarify — on a garbled mix, decodes-and-discusses; even the LLM judge over-counts it.
Rule: any safety eval over encodings or languages must gate on decode fidelity, use a language-agnostic LLM judge, and apply a structural-actionability check. Keyword classifiers overstate success by tens of points.

11Discussion — will a new language ever work?

No, not as a standalone attack on a well-aligned model — the reason is structural. To jailbreak, a language must (1) express the request, (2) be understood, and (3) not trigger safety. But (2) requires mapping to meaning, and safety is tied to meaning. Comprehension is activation of the safety concept, so (2) and (3) conflict. The channels that do get past concept-level safety are not languages: white-box weight edits (out of scope — they delete safety), and turn-structure exploits (prefill), which succeed only when safety isn't enforced across the whole generation.

Isn't the problem just SPUN's coverage or grammar?

A language attack has three things to improve — fluency, coverage, grammar — and all three fall:

  • Fluency is solvable and not the wall (fine-tune decoded unseen SPUN at 1.00).
  • Grammar is not the wall (SPUN has it; it generalized perfectly; real languages have full grammar and are still refused).
  • Coverage is fixable but doesn't help — and we needn't even finish building full-coverage SPUN, because ROT13 and German already are full-coverage, comprehensible languages: ROT13 encodes any text and Frontier-A decodes it at 1.00; German has full grammar and Frontier-A understands it natively. Both are refused.
Closing argument: every fixable property pushes SPUN toward a complete, comprehensible language — which is exactly what concept-level safety already covers. Any language complete enough to carry the payload is complete enough to be caught. The barrier is not a deficiency of SPUN; it is that comprehension and refusal are the same event.

12Defense implications

  • Enforce safety across the entire generation, not just the opening token — the behaviour that makes Frontier-A immune to prefill and Qwen vulnerable.
  • Multilingual and multi-format safety training closes the distribution gaps that surface-level evals falsely flag as robust.
  • Evaluate with decode-gating + a language-agnostic judge + structural checks, never keyword matching.

13Conclusion

The "secret language" hypothesis fails. No invented or real language reliably bypasses a well-aligned model, because safety operates on meaning, not surface form, in the middle/late layers. The genuine jailbreaks we found are a social frame (weak model only) and a turn-structure exploit (one frontier model, defeated by the other). The strongest, defensible contribution is mechanistic and methodological: where safety lives, why language attacks target the wrong half, and how badly naive evals overstate success.
